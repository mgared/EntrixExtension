ORA Front Desk Manual

A keyboard shortcut that writes your shift-log entries for you, so the wording stays the same whoever is on duty. It drafts the line. Reading it is still your job.

02:21 PM: Resident Alvarez from unit (1204) came to the front desk to request entry into their unit because they forgot their keys.

One entry, four clicks. That is the whole idea.

Getting started

Put your cursor where you want the entry, then type two semicolons — ;;. A panel opens at your cursor.

Pick a role (who you are writing about), then a reason (what happened). Fill in the boxes that appear. Watch the preview. Press Enter to insert it, or Esc to close without writing anything.

The two semicolons disappear when the entry goes in — you never have to delete them. Every entry is stamped with the time it was inserted, and starts on its own line.

One thing to do first, though — see Start with Begin shift. Everything that keeps count depends on it.

Where it works

Anywhere you can type inside a web page in Chrome. That covers Outlook on the web and Gmail in a browser tab, which is where the log gets written.

Not in the desktop Outlook app

The extension is part of the browser, so it cannot reach into a program running outside it. Open the Outlook app and type ;; and nothing happens — not a fault, just out of reach.

If that is where you write your logs, open Outlook in a Chrome tab instead.

Getting back to this page

Guide, along the bottom of the panel, opens this manual in a new tab. Nothing you have typed is lost — the panel stays open behind it.

Start with Begin shift

Press it before anything else

Everything the extension keeps count of is measured from the moment you press Begin shift. Without it there is no shift to measure against, so the tracking has nothing to stand on — it does not guess, it just stays quiet.

You can still use the extension without starting a shift, and most of it works exactly the same. It is worth knowing which half is which:

What goes wrong quietly

None of these announce themselves, which is why they are worth knowing:

  • The task chips just sit plain. Not green, not red — plain. That looks like "nothing due", not like "not tracking". No badge and no notifications either.
  • A flagged entry is highlighted but not filed. The yellow tint still lands on your line, but the copy has nowhere to go: Begin shift is what writes the section headings into the log, and without them the filing simply does not happen. Nothing warns you.
  • Still out keeps whatever was already on it. Handing keys over still adds them and returning them still takes them off, so the list works — but it is never cleared, so it can be showing you a previous shift's keys alongside yours. Begin shift is what empties it.

So: log whatever you like without starting a shift — the entries themselves are just as good. Only don't read anything into the task colours, the badge or the Still out list until a shift has been started.

Check every line before you move on

This is the one rule that matters

The tool writes a draft. It does not know what actually happened — only what you picked from the menus. After every insert, read the line back and fix it if it is not exactly right.

A log is a record. If a line says something that did not happen, that is on the person whose name is at the top of the shift, not on the extension.

Things worth checking every time:

  • Is the unit number right? A wrong unit is the easiest mistake to make and the hardest to spot later.
  • Does the sentence claim something you did not do? If it says a team was notified, make sure you notified them.
  • Are there #### marks left? Those are blanks you have not filled in. Never leave them in a finished log.
  • Does it fit what really happened? If the closest reason is only close, insert it and then edit the wording. That is faster than typing from scratch, and it is still your sentence.

And when nothing in the menus fits at all — just type the entry yourself. The extension is there to save you time on the ninety per cent that repeats, not to stop you writing. See What it can't do.

The popup

Reading top to bottom:

  • Role — who the entry is about. Resident, Guest, Package, Vendor, Cleaner, Maintenance, Concierge and so on.
  • The boxes beside it — these change with the role. A resident entry asks for a name, a unit, and whether they came to the desk or called in.
  • Reason — what happened. The list changes depending on the role you picked.
  • More boxes — some reasons ask for extra detail. They appear under the reason once you choose it.
  • Preview — the exact sentence that will be inserted. It updates as you type. If the preview is wrong, the log will be wrong.
  • Chips — one-click entries that skip the role and reason entirely. Covered below.

Name is optional almost everywhere. If you did not catch a name, leave it blank and the sentence simply will not mention one.

Filling in blanks

If you insert an entry with a required box still empty, the sentence goes in with a #### in that spot rather than a made-up value:

Inserted with the unit left blank 02:21 PM: Resident from unit (####) came to the front desk to request entry into their unit because they forgot their keys.

That is deliberate — a visible gap you must fill beats a quiet guess. The first blank is selected as soon as the entry lands, so you can type straight into it. Press Tab to jump to the next blank and Shift + Tab to go back. Once you type over a blank it stops being one.

Names in the shift skeleton come in red for the same reason: so the people on and off site stand out in a page that is otherwise a wall of timestamps.

The four notify boxes

Ticking one of these does two things:

  1. Highlights the entry in yellow, so anyone scanning the log spots it.
  2. Files a second copy of it further down the log, under the matching section heading.

You can tick more than one — the entry is filed under each heading you pick. The filing only works if those headings already exist in the log, which they will if the shift was started with Begin shift.

The note box

Ticking any box opens What should they expect or do? underneath. Whatever you write there is added to the filed copy only — not to the line at your cursor. It is the place for the bit the next team needs that does not belong in the timeline.

Write it as more detail about this entry, aimed at the team you ticked — what they are walking into, or what you need them to do. It reads as one note with the entry it belongs to, so it has to follow on from it.

That one entry lands in two places, highlighted in both:

In the timeline 02:59 PM: Resident Alvarez from unit (1204) came to the front desk to report that their kitchen sink is leaking.
And under FACILITY/MAINTENANCE NOTES * 02:59 PM: Resident Alvarez from unit (1204) came to the front desk to report that their kitchen sink is leaking. Water is pooling under the cabinet; the resident has put a bucket down and asks for someone first thing tomorrow.

The filed copy is the same sentence with your note on the end. Only the copy carries the note — the timeline stays a clean record of what happened, when.

Chips, one by one

Chips are the entries you write over and over. They skip the role and reason menus. A chip with a … after its name opens a short form first; the rest insert straight away.

Quick logs

Time

Drops in just the timestamp and leaves your cursor after it.

Use it for anything you are going to type yourself. It saves you looking at the clock and keeps the format identical to every other line.

Key returned

The only chip that does not start a new line. It adds “Key returned” to the end of the entry your cursor is already sitting on.

Use it for closing off the line where you logged the key going out, so the handing-out and the coming-back read as one event.

Begin shift…

Builds the entire skeleton for your shift: the header, an empty line for every hour, and the standing note sections. Full details below.

Use it for the first thing you do when you sit down.

Still out…

Lists everything the desk has lent out and not got back. Also the one place to mark something as returned. Full details below.

Use it for the handover, and any time you need to know who has what.

End shift…

Writes the closing line — you off site, your relief on site — and asks whether you handed the concierge keys over. It also stops the task deadlines.

Use it for the last thing you do before you leave. If you skip it, the task chips stay red forever.

Night lockup

Inserts the full lockup wording: lobby quiet, music down, lights dimmed, vestibules checked and secured, back at the desk.

Use it for the overnight round. Read it back — if you did not actually dim the lights, edit the line.

Lobby checked

A one-line “lobby inspected, all clear”.

Use it for a quick look round that does not need the full site tour.

Tasks

These three do the same job as a quick log, but the extension also keeps count and colours the chip by how you are doing.

Begin shift

Fill in three things and tick one box:

  • Your name and Off-going — who is coming on and who is leaving.
  • Shift — which rota you are on. This sets the hour lines and every task deadline for the rest of the shift.
  • Concierge keys received — tick it if you took the keys, and the opening line says so.

What you get is a ready-made page: the header line, a blank timestamped line for every hour of your shift, and the standing sections at the bottom waiting to be filled.

Begin shift also resets the tracking

Starting a shift wipes the task counts and the Still out list. That is on purpose — you inherit no ticked tasks and no key list from the last person.

So use it once, at the start. Pressing it again mid-shift will clear everything you have logged as still out.

Site tour

The checklist is grouped by floor, in the order the walk is done — 1st floor, 2nd floor, 11th floor, then 12th floor. The floor heading stays on screen while you scroll, so you always know which one you are looking at.

One row per area, and everything on a row is optional:

  • The tick box — says what you confirmed. For most areas that is “all clear”; some say something more specific, like secured on the emergency exit door or organized and neat on the meeting spaces. The coffee machines also carry a status dropdown, because servicing them is part of the walk.
  • Something to report — anything that was not right.
  • A headcount on the pool and grill areas.

An area you do not touch is not mentioned at all, and neither is a floor you did not walk — its heading simply does not appear in the sentence. That is the point: the log only ever claims what you actually looked at. If you only got round half the building, only fill in that half.

All clear and a note cancel each other out

Those two contradict each other, so the form will not let you have both. Start typing a note and the tick clears itself and greys out. Empty the note and the tick comes back — but it does not tick itself again, because whether the area was clear is your call, not the form's.

What it writes

Still one line, however many floors you covered. Each floor is named once and its areas follow it:

The whole walk, one line 02:11 PM: Site tour completed. 1st floor - Lobby area: waiting area chairs organized, all clear; Mail room: all clear; Genius bar: all clear; Leasing Office: all clear; Meeting spaces: organized and neat; Coffee machine #1: all clear, stocked and rinsed; GYM: treadmill out of order; Dog wash: clean; Emergency exit door: secured; Freight elevator and second lobby: all clear. 2nd floor - Courtyard: all clear; Trash Chute by Moxies: all clear. 11th floor - Electrical room: music turned on and operational. 12th floor - Reservable patio and area: all clear; TV and game room: TV turned on, pool table and shuffle board set; Coffee machine #2: all clear; Inner space: all clear; Fireplace: turned off and all clear; Pool area: all clear (3 people); Grill area: all clear.

Anything you reported comes out in bold, so it does not get lost in the middle of a long line.

Still out

Things get onto this list by themselves. When you log a reason that hands something over — keys to a cleaner, a dolly to a resident, vendor keys — the extension adds it. When you log the matching return, it comes off.

This form is the backstop for everything else. Tick Returned next to anything that has come back, and it is off the list whichever way it was logged out.

Insert it at handover and the next person sees exactly what is outstanding. If nothing is out, it says so rather than leaving an empty heading.

The ID rule

If you hand over a key that has to come back — a master key, a spare, a vendor key — you hold an ID. No exceptions. The reason wordings say “exchanged for an ID” because that is what happened.

The one case where you do not hold an ID: a key the resident owns and left at the desk for someone to collect and keep.

Tasks it chases you about

Three chips are on a clock — Site tour, Read previous shift logs and Desk organized. Once you have started a shift, the extension counts them and chases you when one falls behind. Nothing else in the extension notifies you about anything.

The badge on the toolbar icon

You do not have to keep the panel open — or even be looking at the log. When a task goes overdue, a red count appears on the extension's icon in the browser toolbar and stays there until the task is done:

Apex Shift Log Helper 2  · two tasks overdue right now.

The badge is the signal that cannot be missed or dismissed. It is not affected by Do Not Disturb, and it does not go away on its own. Tick the task off and it clears straight away.

The desktop notification

A notification also pops up the moment a deadline passes, and again every half hour while the task is still outstanding — once and never again is too easy to swipe away and forget. This is what it says:

Shift task overdue
Site tour: 2 still needed before the shift ends.
Google Chrome · Apex Shift Log Helper

One per overdue task. Clicking it brings the window you were working in back to the front.

If you never see a notification

Check two things, in this order:

  1. Did you press Begin shift? Deadlines are worked out from the shift you picked there. No shift started means no deadlines, no badge and no notifications — the chips just sit plain.
  2. Are notifications allowed for Chrome? If the computer is set to Do Not Disturb, or Chrome is not permitted to show notifications, the pop-up never appears. The badge still works, so watch the toolbar icon.

When the deadlines fall

They come from the shift you picked in Begin shift. The first site tour is due inside your first two hours; Read previous shift logs inside your first hour. Later ones have to close an hour before your shift ends, so nothing is left to land in the handover.

Press End shift when you leave. It stops the clock. Skip it and the deadlines keep running after you have gone home — the badge sits on the icon overnight and the next person inherits red chips that mean nothing.

What it can't do

The menus cover the shifts that repeat. They will never cover everything, and they are not meant to. Expect to write entries yourself — that is normal use, not a failure.

Things you will have to type

  • Anything unusual. A fire alarm, a medical call, a police visit, a flood, a lift entrapment. Rare things do not belong in a menu you scroll past forty times a day.
  • Conversations with any nuance. A complaint with a history to it, a dispute between neighbours, anything where the wording matters more than the facts.
  • Anything you would want quoted back accurately. If it might be read by a lawyer or an insurer, write it in your own words.
  • Follow-ups. The tool logs an event; it does not know that this is the third time the same resident has asked.

Things it deliberately will not say

  • It will not raise a maintenance request. A concierge cannot file one. The most the wording will claim is that you gave the resident the Emergency Maintenance number to call themselves.
  • It will not claim a team was notified unless you say so. Reasons that involve leasing or maintenance ask what actually happened — including “could not be reached”, because that is the outcome the next shift most needs to know.

What it gets wrong

  • Close-but-not-right wording. Insert the nearest reason and edit it. Still faster than typing the whole line.
  • Both copies of a flagged entry are word-for-word identical. If the maintenance team needs different detail than the concierge team, use the note box — or edit the filed copy afterwards.
  • It does not know what you did while it was closed. Task counts only move when you press the chip.

Same rule, one more time

Read every line after it goes in. The extension is a typist, not a witness — it writes what you selected, not what happened.
