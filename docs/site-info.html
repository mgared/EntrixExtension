Starting your shift

  1. Log into Connecteam. Check updates, chat and announcements.
  2. Start your assigned checklist.
  3. Read the previous shift log in full.
  4. Copy the bottom section of that log into yours, then update it and clear anything no longer outstanding.

Greet and assist anyone entering the building. Presence at the desk is the job as much as anything written down here.

Deliveries: the 15/30 rule

Every delivery, unless told otherwise

15 minutes — call the resident.
30 minutes — take it off the desk, store it, log it.

Where it goes at 30 minutes depends on what it is:

At 30 minutesAfter that
Fast food Refrigerator, logged 24 h — discard
Grocery / meal prep Refrigerated storage, labelled, logged as a perishable package 24 h — call the resident, advise immediate pickup
48 h — notify leasing to follow up
Non-perishable Package room, logged as a package Normal package handling
Flowers, gifts Call the resident immediately, do not wait out the 15 minutes

If the fridge is full, call the resident at 30 minutes, tell them refrigerated storage is unavailable, and hold the item aside. Perishables still expire at 24 hours either way.

Drivers going upstairs

Never on the driver's word alone. If a driver asks to deliver to the door, call the resident. If the resident confirms, grant elevator access. If nobody answers, the driver does not go up and the delivery stays at the desk.

If the resident calls ahead instead, check the number they are calling from matches their profile, then grant access.

Log it

Every call, every item stored, every item discarded, every time the fridge was full, and every driver granted access. If it is not in the log it did not happen.

Bell carts and dollies

Two bell carts and two red dollies, kept in the back package room. They sign out exactly like a key:

  1. Collect a photo ID.
  2. Record it on the sign-out sheet under the desk.
  3. Hand over the equipment.
  4. On return: inspect it, log the return, give the ID back.

No ID, no equipment. There is no version of this where you skip the ID.

Tell management or maintenance if

  • Equipment is missing or damaged.
  • The pool is over capacity.
  • You see unauthorized access, or the bike room security is compromised.
  • An amenity is being misused.

Keys, IDs and lockouts

Never hand out a master key

For a lockout: verify three identifiers or a valid ID, then escort the resident yourself using the main key. The key does not leave your hand.

Releasing a key

  1. Collect a photo ID.
  2. Log it in the key binder.
  3. Return the ID when the key comes back.

This applies to spare fobs, vendor keys, dollies and bell carts too.

Noise complaints

You are an observer, not an enforcer

Never knock on a unit door about noise. Never enter a unit, never argue, never try to enforce anything yourself. If a resident confronts you: stay neutral, refer to policy, disengage.

Skip everything and call the police if you hear

Fighting or threats · a domestic disturbance · anything that sounds like illegal activity · property being damaged · anything that makes you fear for someone's safety.

Those do not get a courtesy call. They get authorities, immediately.

Otherwise, four steps

Step
1 · Verify Walk discreetly past the unit. Is it audible from the hallway or a common area? If you cannot verify it, document the complaint and keep an eye on it.
2 · Courtesy call Once confirmed, or once you have had more than one complaint, call the resident.
3 · Warning If it continues, call again and say plainly that the next step is contacting local authorities.
4 · Police If it still continues, or another complaint comes in, call the non-emergency line. Once the threshold is met this is not a judgement call.

What to say

Courtesy call:

Hello, this is the concierge desk. We received a noise concern and as a courtesy we're asking that volume be lowered out of respect for neighboring residents. Thank you for your cooperation.

Warning:

We have received an additional complaint regarding noise. If it continues, the next step will be to contact local authorities to address the matter.

State it calmly. It is policy, not a threat, and it is not personal.

What never to say

Not "someone complained about you". Not "you need to be quiet". Not "you're violating the rules". Always "we received a concern" — it keeps the complaint anonymous and keeps you out of the middle of it.

Log every one

In E.D.O. format: the time the complaint came in, the unit, whether you could verify it, every call you made, the outcome, and whether police were contacted.

Writing the log

Your log should tell the story of the shift start to finish. Anyone reading it should come away knowing what happened, what you did, and what still needs follow-up. It is read by the concierge team, Apex leadership, property management, leasing and maintenance, and decisions get made from it.

E.D.O.

Every entry:

  • E — Event. What happened.
  • D — Detail. Relevant facts only.
  • O — Outcome. Resolution, status, or next step.

Tone

Objective, factual, concise, neutral. No opinions, no emotional language, no assumptions, no speculation. If a concern is personal, take it to leadership directly rather than putting it in the log.

Anything unresolved

Give the full context, what you already did, and what follow-up is needed. The next shift has to be able to pick it up without asking you.

Submitting

At the end of every shift, to the email group PASS ON LOG — concierge team, management, leasing, maintenance and leadership. If someone else needs to see a particular log, CC them and tell leadership so they can be added permanently.

End every log with your name, property, shift time and date.
