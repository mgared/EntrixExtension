Shifts and the log

Three shifts: 7am-3pm · 3pm-11pm · 11pm-7am.

Every shift is logged from the moment it starts. Begin the log with the header and the hour lines, work down it through the shift, and close it when your relief arrives. The trailing sections carry anything the next shift or another team needs to pick up:

  • KEYS REMAINING OUT
  • MOVE IN/MOVE OUT KEYS
  • PROPERTY MANAGEMENT NOTES
  • CONCIERGE TEAM NOTES
  • FACILITY/MAINTENANCE NOTES
  • INCIDENTS OF NOTE

Two rules that hold whatever else changes. Log it when it happens, not at the end of the shift from memory. And write what happened, not what should have happened — a log is a record, and it may be read months later by someone deciding what went wrong.

Keys and IDs

An ID is held for every key that has to come back

Master keys, spare keys, vendor keys: if the desk hands it over and expects it back, you hold an ID until it is returned. There is no exception.

The one case where no ID is held: a key the resident owns and left at the desk for someone to collect and keep. Nothing is coming back, so there is nothing to hold against.

Log the key going out and the key coming back. Anything still out at the end of your shift belongs in the handover, by name and unit, so the next person knows who has what.

Letting people up

Nobody goes up unconfirmed. Confirmation is either the resident on the phone, the resident's visitor list, or an earlier request the resident already made. If none of those, they do not go up, and the log says the desk could not reach the resident.

  • Guests are confirmed with the resident, then given elevator access.
  • Cleaners and dog walkers are confirmed the same way. Keys are exchanged for an ID.
  • Vendors may be working for a resident or for the building, so they are confirmed by whoever has authority over the space: the resident, or the leasing or maintenance team.
Fill in
  • Where the visitor list lives and who can add to it.
  • Whether guests are escorted, and after what hour.
  • What to do about someone who refuses to be announced.
  • Contractor rules: working hours, insurance, sign-in.

Maintenance

The desk does not raise maintenance requests

A concierge cannot file one. When a resident reports something, give them the Emergency Maintenance number and have them make the call themselves, then log that you did so.

Do not promise a response time. If it is out of hours, say plainly that a same-evening response cannot be guaranteed.

What the desk can do is record it and flag it, so the maintenance team picks it up from the log.

  • What counts as an emergency versus a next-day job.
  • Who to call for a flood, a lift entrapment, or a power failure.
  • Whether the desk holds a shut-off or alarm panel key.

Packages and deliveries

Packages are logged in when they arrive and logged out when they are collected. Food and app deliveries are different: the courier hands over or the resident comes down, and the log records what actually happened rather than assuming the resident was reached.

The loading dock

Residents book the dock through the desk. Take the date, the time slot, and what they want it for. The desk reserves it directly; there are two outcomes, the slot was taken or it was already gone.

When they come down or call to say they are ready, set the dock and the elevator up and log that you did. Both go in the shift log, hours or days apart. The dock is also walked on the site tour, which is a separate thing again: just the check that it is in order.
