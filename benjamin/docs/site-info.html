Letting people up

Dog walkers, housekeepers, babysitters, trainers: it all comes down to whether they are on the guest list and whether PTE (permission to enter) is checked.

SituationWhat you do
On the list, PTE checked Grant access. No call needed
On the list, PTE not checked Call the resident for verbal permission
Not on the list Call the resident for direct authorization

No answer means no access

Every time. It does not matter how convincing the person at the desk is, how long they have been waiting, or how often they have been here before.

Deliveries

A driver saying the resident told them to come up is not authorization. Confirm it yourself, every time.

  • The driver says the resident instructed them — call the resident anyway. No answer, the delivery stays at the desk.
  • The resident calls down first — check the number they are calling from matches the one on file. If it does, that is verbal PTE: send the driver up, and log it.

How to put it to the resident:

We have your delivery at the desk, would you like me to send them up?

When in doubt, hold it at the desk. That is the version of this you never have to explain afterwards.

Packages into a unit

Rare, not routine

Do not offer PTE as a solution. Putting packages inside a unit is for extreme cases only: overflow that has overwhelmed storage, oversized items, a pre-move-in shipment.

Start by calling the resident. "Are you available to receive this today?" If not, when will they be?

Case
Not moved in yetEscalate to leasing
Moving in within 1–3 daysYou may accommodate it temporarily
Longer than 3 days outEscalate to leasing for direction
Extended absenceThe resident may email written PTE. Move packages in only if it is safe and reasonable. Case by case

Never move more than you can safely carry. If overflow is beating the storage or the porters, tell leasing rather than working around it. Log every step.

Noise complaints

You are an observer, not an enforcer

Never knock on a unit door about noise. Never enter, never argue, never try to enforce it yourself. If a resident confronts you: stay neutral, refer to policy, disengage.

Skip everything and call the police if you hear

Fighting or threats · a domestic disturbance · anything that sounds like illegal activity · property being damaged · anything that makes you fear for someone's safety.

Otherwise, four steps

1 · VerifyWalk discreetly past. Audible from the hallway or a common area? If you cannot verify it, document it and monitor
2 · Courtesy callOnce confirmed, or once more than one complaint has come in
3 · WarningIf it continues, say plainly that the next step is contacting local authorities
4 · PoliceNon-emergency line. Once the threshold is met this is not a judgement call

Courtesy call:

Hello, this is the concierge desk. We received a noise concern and as a courtesy we're asking that volume be lowered out of respect for neighboring residents. Thank you for your cooperation.

Warning:

We have received an additional complaint regarding noise. If it continues, the next step will be to contact local authorities to address the matter.

Never "someone complained about you", never "you need to be quiet", never "you're violating the rules". Always "we received a concern" — it keeps the complaint anonymous and you out of the middle of it.

Incident reports

Connecteam → Forms → Incident Report. It walks you through the questions so nothing gets missed.

Add PDaSilva@berkshireresi.com when you submit. Apex management is on it automatically.

File one immediately for

  • Accidents or injuries — staff, resident or guest, on site.
  • Property damage — the building's or someone's own.
  • Security breach — unauthorized access, theft, anything that got past a control.
  • Complaints or disputes — anything management needs to pick up.

Be specific and factual, check the recipients before sending, and do it now rather than at the end of the shift.

Writing the log

Anyone reading your log should come away knowing what happened, what you did, and what still needs follow-up. Every entry:

  • E — Event. What happened.
  • D — Detail. Relevant facts only.
  • O — Outcome. Resolution, status, or next step.

Objective, factual, concise, neutral. No opinions, no assumptions, no speculation. If a concern is personal, take it to leadership rather than putting it in the log.
